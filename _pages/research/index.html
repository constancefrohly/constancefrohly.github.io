Works in Progress

Asymetry in Belief Formation about Public and Private Middle Schools in France

This project focuses on how families form beliefs about public and private middle schools in France. I document asymetries in households’ belief accuracy regarding school quality, class size and peer composition, depending on their characteristics and in particular whether they have children in private and/or public schools. Preliminary evidence suggests that parents with children enrolled in private schools substantially underestimate the quality of public schools, while displaying similar levels of accuracy regarding private schools, relative to parents with children enrolled in public schools. The project combines experimental and administrative data to investigate whether parents develop motivated beliefs about middle schools in France.

Private School Entry and Its Spillovers on Public Middle Schools: Segregation and Performance in France

This project studies how the entry and expansion of private middle schools affect neighboring public schools in France. I find that the opening of a new private school attracts advantaged students from nearby public schools and thereby increases social segregation. In addition, lower performing students remaining in public schools see a decrease in their academic performance at the national exam at 15 years old (Brevet), which is consistent with reduced peer effects within public schools following the increase in segregation.

Long-Term Effects of Omissions in Applications to Higher Education: Evidence from Denmark

with A. Bjerre-Nielsen, E. Chrisander

This project analyzes the determinants and the long-term consequences of misreporting preferences in college applications. Using Danish administrative data, our preliminary findings show that omissions are mostly due to pessimistic admission beliefs, and have small but significant effects on students’ future academic performance and employment.
